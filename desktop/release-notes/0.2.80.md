# WING CONTROLLER Desktop v0.2.80

Build: 20261005.2 (macOS Apple Silicon)
Date: 2026-10-05

The cue system, finished as a system rather than a list.

## The clock window

- **A chevron expands the cue panel** and the window grows and remembers the height you left it at. When the frame
  refuses to grow, the panel **says so** and tells you why: the window is already maximised, or this display's usable
  height is the ceiling, with the number.
- **Cues are listed in timecode order by default**, with a selector for manual or creation order, and the choice is saved
  **in the cue file**, so opening the same show elsewhere keeps it.
- **A cue's time is editable**: four fields, hours to frames, with a nudge of one or ten frames. Editing is local; saving
  is explicit, and the button says that saving **re-arms** the list.
- **The readout and the countdown move continuously**, interpolated between snapshots instead of stepping four times a
  second.

## The cue list itself

- **Your own name for a cue**, separate from the macro's note.
- **Numbers you can say out loud**, as decimals: between 5 and 6 an operator puts 5.5 and nothing else moves. The number
  lives inside the cue, reordering does not touch it, and **renumbering cannot break a firing triggered from outside**,
  because what an outside system addresses is the cue's id.
- **Groups** for a long show: a free name per cue, never an address, so a group never changes what fires.
- **Search, lock, duplicate and undo**, and a **"what just happened"** line with the position, the name and the wall
  clock, plus warnings ten and five seconds before the next cue.
- **Broken cues are marked** instead of failing quietly: a cue naming no macro, or a macro the library does not have.

## Reliability, which is the part that is not visible

- **A cue can no longer be consumed without its macro running, silently.** The engine records what it decided before
  delivering anything and keeps it until the window confirms what actually happened; a refused hand-over is retried, and
  the counts are visible in the clock and in Settings, with a warning colour when something is unconfirmed or lost.
- **Cues fire after restarting the application**, which they did not: the saved list is installed at startup now.
- **A corrupt cue list is repaired from its own recovery copy.**
- **The list can be exported to a file and imported back**, and importing validates the file **before** replacing
  anything, so an invalid one leaves your list untouched.
- **A station backup now carries the cue list.** Restoring a show used to leave the cues behind.

## Also in this release

- The LTC input accepts **29.97** and its drop-frame numbering. The rate selector offers it.
- The interface no longer promises "LTC from a file": a file is **checked**, never played.
- Two flaky test failures were diagnosed to their real causes and fixed, and the verification gate can no longer run
  twice at once on one machine, which was manufacturing failures that belonged to nobody.

## Known limits

- The cue row is written once and used by both windows, and both suites pin it so they cannot drift apart.
- Which macro a cue should fire is stored as its place in the library. After importing a different library, a cue can
  point at a different macro without anything being able to detect it; a cue pointing past the end of the library is
  marked.
- Timecode from a real LTC signal is verified at the desk. The acceptance pass with Mixing Station still needs the desk.
