# WING CONTROLLER Desktop v0.2.93

Build: 20261007.2 (macOS Apple Silicon)
Date: 2026-10-07

## The courtesy notice is yours to size

The floating card that confirms a macro fired was one size, in one colour, for a fixed 3.5 seconds. It is now
adjustable, in **Properties → HUD & Courtesy Notices**:

- **Auto-dismiss after** — a slider from half a second to thirty. Fully left is **Persistent**: the card then waits
  for its ✕ instead of leaving on its own. The thin bar along the bottom drains over exactly the time you chose, so
  the card itself says how long it has left.
- **Notice size** — from 70 % to 180 %. The text and the padding grow; the borders stay exactly one pixel, so a large
  card still reads as a classic window rather than a stretched picture.
- **Accent colour** — the caption and the drain bar.
- **Flash the card** — off, or only when an action failed, or on every notice.

Both sliders apply as you drag them, so the card you are describing is the card on screen, and **» Test Courtesy
Notice** draws a real one without waiting for a show.

## Why the colour does not reach a failure

A card says what happened by its colour: **red** means an action failed, **blue** means you fired a test, and the
accent is what an ordinary notice wears. The accent deliberately does **not** touch the first two. A preference that
could repaint a failure into an ordinary-looking notice would turn the card into a report that lies, and a report that
lies is worse than no report at all — so a failed action keeps its red caption and a test keeps its blue one, and the
setting says so on its own tooltip.

## The flash, and what it will not do

The flash pulses the card's outline **once a second**. That is deliberate: a surface that flashes more than about
three times a second is a photosensitivity risk, and this setting is meant to catch the eye, not to be a hazard. It is
off unless you ask for it, it can be limited to failures, and it is switched off entirely when the system is set to
reduce motion.

## Under the hood

The size is applied as a scale on the type and the padding rather than as a bitmap transform on the card, which is
why the one-pixel borders stay crisp at 180 %. The accent is validated before it reaches the stylesheet, because it
ends up in a CSS variable and a value read back from storage must never be able to inject rules. The caption's ink is
computed from the accent's luminance, so a pale colour cannot give you an unreadable title.

Your existing settings keep working: the older duration choices (2 s, 3.5 s, 5 s) land on the new slider unchanged,
and the corner, the chime and "enable for new macros" are where they were.
