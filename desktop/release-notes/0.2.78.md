# WING CONTROLLER Desktop v0.2.78

Build: 20261004.8 (macOS Apple Silicon)
Date: 2026-10-04

A release about a cue that could disappear without saying anything.

## What you will notice

- **A cue can no longer be consumed without its macro running, silently.** The engine marks a cue as spent when the
  timecode crosses it — it has to, or the show would fire it twice — but that only means the engine decided. The
  decision is now written down with a receipt **before** anything is delivered, and it stays there until the window
  confirms what it actually did. If the hand-over is refused, it is retried on the next pass.
- **You can see it.** Cues fired, delivered, blocked, with nothing to do, failed, still unconfirmed, retried and lost,
  in the clock window and in Settings — in a warning colour when something is unconfirmed or lost.
- **Your cues now fire after restarting the application.** They were only being installed into the engine when you
  saved or edited the list, so relaunching left the engine with no cues at all: nothing fired, and nothing said why.

## Behind the scenes

- The channel that carried the firing order is now just a doorbell. The order never travels through it, so a full queue
  can no longer drop it.
- The listener is registered before the first wait at startup, and whatever fired while the window was not listening is
  picked up afterwards.

## Known limits, written down rather than hidden

- **A reload right between firing and confirming can repeat a macro.** Not losing a cue was chosen over never repeating
  one. If that ever shows up, the alternative is remembering confirmations on disk.
- **A station backup does not carry the cue list.** Restoring a show on another machine leaves the cues behind.
- **The cue list's own backup copy is never re-read**: if the main file is corrupt, the window says so, but the good
  copy beside it is not used yet.
- **Importing a macro library can re-point a cue at a different macro** without anything noticing, if the indices move.

## Still not proved

Timecode from a real LTC signal is now proved at the desk; what remains is the acceptance pass with Mixing Station,
which needs the desk itself.
