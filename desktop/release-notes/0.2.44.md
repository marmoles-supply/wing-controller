# WING CONTROLLER Desktop v0.2.44

Build: 20260922.17 (macOS Apple Silicon)
Date: 2026-09-22

The complete guide, inside the app and in its own window.

## What it is

The **Guide & Help** tab of Properties stays as a **brief summary** (what each block does and the two steps
to get started) and gains a button, **Open the complete guide**, which opens the full guide in **a separate
window**: it can be kept next to the working window, on a second monitor.

## First: the alert cannot take away your PANIC STOP

Reported live: a macro with a HUD alert and sound that fired much more often than its alert lasts left the
card stuck on screen —a new beep on every shot— and the panic button ended up under the alert. You could
not even get to disconnect.

- **With an alert on screen, the header floats above it**: PANIC STOP, the status chip and the Stop button
  still receive the click.
- **The alert card brings its own PANIC STOP**, through the same path as the header's.
- **PANIC STOP** (either of the two paths, or `F12`) clears the alert out of the way and lets no further
  alerts through for 3 s; each one ignored is logged as `hud_alert_ignored_after_panic`.
- **The avalanche is capped**: an identical repeated alert is grouped and counted on screen
  (`Repeating ×N`, with the copies from a burst that were discarded), the beeps run at most one per second
  with a single `AudioContext` for the whole session and fall silent from the fifth repetition onwards, and
  after three times its own duration the card freezes —no flash, no countdown and no sound— until someone
  closes it. A **different** alert is always painted immediately: the information is not swallowed.
- The PANIC confirm (if it is enabled) is now also painted above the alert, and the bridge carries the same
  engine and the same button in its card.
- The **courtesy alerts** follow the same path: the card of the same macro is replaced instead of stacking,
  there are at most three on screen and the little bell rings at most once per second. The three sounds of
  the window (alert beep, bell and panic acknowledgement) now share a single `AudioContext` instead of
  creating one per sound.

## Eleven chapters, by topic

1. **Start here** — what it is and what it is not, the two windows, the two steps, the safety model.
2. **Triggers** — the two families (telemetry and console state), the fine tuning of the meter triggers
   (transient/level edge, cooldown, rearm, dwell, stale), baseline and first edge, IF gates.
3. **Actions & timing** — the seven step modes and, above all, **what happens in what order and at what
   speed**: the timeline is sequential, a numeric `Set` re-reads the sheet (up to ~250 ms) and warns if it
   does not match, the global limiter is 17 ms per packet, ramps go from 2 to 128 packets (20 ms up to
   ~2.5 s and coarser after that: 60 s = 472 ms per packet), 32 steps per macro, delays and fades up to
   60 s, and what happens when two ramps overlap (full queue = failed macro with steps not executed).
4. **Macros & limits** — the library (cap of 128, silent truncation), why a macro cannot be enabled, arm vs
   enable, preflight, Verify Targets, labels and courtesy alert.
5. **Meters & banks** — watchlist and aliases, the styles, the zoom, peak/CLIP, "Only meters", and the
   banks shared with the bridge with an independent choice per window (and why a bank wider than the
   watchlist is still shown and subscribed).
6. **Saving & scopes** — what is saved and where, the **scopes system** (macros / settings / meters /
   network) with the keys of each one, the schema 3 envelope, the per-section restore that deletes
   nothing, show profiles, recovery copies.
7. **Protocol (technical)** — the three channels and their ports, the native frames (`0xdf`, `0xd3`,
   `0xd4`, `0xdc`/`0xde`, `0xa0…0xaf`), the `report id`, the 1/256 dB and its exceptions (V2 states 0/1,
   FX scale ×6/2048), the meter groups, subscription and renewal, the sheet reads, and **what the app does
   not support** with the reason.
8. **Diagnostics** — every number on the card explained (unit, window, healthy value and what to suspect),
   the trigger latency, the OSC monitor and its filters, the log and the probes.
9. **Troubleshooting** — symptom → causes in order → where to look → what to do, with the real cases (it
   does not connect, it does not fire, it fires but nothing happens, the meters stop, the lock blocks, the
   bank does not filter, PANIC does not undo).
10. **Shortcuts & UI** — all the shortcuts of the three windows with the window they work in, the
    Inspector, the context menus, the Properties tabs, density and theme.
11. **Recipes & a show in practice** — five moments of a show day (soundcheck, doors, concert, act change,
    closing) and ten recipes ready to type, each with its "watch out for this", plus a table of limits at a
    glance and five things that cannot be done today.

## Also

- **Three Inspector help texts no longer promise more than the code does**: "Fade start" said the runtime
  reads the parameter before ramping (a Fade never reads: it sends the values you wrote), "Decode" promised
  "typically below 50 µs" (it is the last measurement and it goes up if the Mac is busy) and the trigger
  latency said "emitted to the window" (the clock stops when the action is handed to the actions thread,
  before the OSC write and the painting). The guide had to contradict all three.
- **The graph speed no longer promises seconds.** The label said "seconds on screen" and the number is a
  speed (1x…10x) which also means different things in each window: the bridge draws `12000 / speed` ms of
  history, and the dock pushes one sample every `350 / speed` ms onto a fixed buffer of 60. Now both
  windows label 1x…10x and the tooltip says how much history that is in that window.

The guide has a **search box** (it filters chapters and highlights matches), an **index**, **hash links** (a
chapter can be linked), **printing** (all in one go, for PDF), it remembers its size and position, and it
follows the app theme.

## How it is maintained

The content lives in `docs/guide/*.md` (reviewable source, 14,102 words, in English, with the `file:line`
citation for every technical claim). It is converted to `desktop/ui/guide-content.js` with
`node scripts/generate-guide-content.mjs`, and **two contracts watch it**: one fails the gate if the
generated file falls behind or if a chapter comes out empty or with unrendered Markdown, and the other
drives the window in Chrome (chapters, content, index, links and search).

## What to try

1. **Properties → Guide & Help**: the summary must be short and the **Open the complete guide** button must
   open the guide window.
2. **The guide**: change chapter in the index; paste `#actions-and-timing` in the address bar (or use the
   hash) and see that it opens that chapter.
3. **The search box**: type `panic` or `scope` — only the chapters that mention it must remain, with the
   matches highlighted; the ✕ clears it.
4. **Print**: the whole guide should come out, chapter after chapter.
5. **Resize and close**: when reopened it must remember size and position; the light/dark theme must follow
   the main window's.
6. **Alert avalanche (what you reported)**: arm a macro with a HUD step (Center HUD, with sound, duration
   5 s) and a trigger that fires very easily, with Cooldown 0. When it fires in a loop, the card must group
   (`Repeating ×N`, with the burst copies discarded), there must not be a beep per shot and after ~15 s it
   must go still (no flash or countdown). With the alert on top, the three stops must work: the card
   button, the header one and `F12` —also with the bridge window in the foreground—; after the stop the
   alert disappears and does not come back.
